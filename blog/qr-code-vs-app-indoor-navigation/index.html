QR code vs app: which indoor navigation works better for venues?

By Areyos · 2026-10-23

Venues that want to help visitors find their way indoors usually weigh two approaches: a dedicated mobile app, or a QR code that opens a map in the phone's browser.

The problem with apps

Most venue visitors come rarely. A patient, a parent at an admissions day or a shopper at a new mall is unlikely to download an app for one visit. Installs need storage, time and a good connection, which is a lot to ask at the moment someone is already stressed or in a hurry.

How QR-based navigation differs

  • No install: scan with the native camera and the map opens in the browser.
  • Instant access: the QR sits exactly where people get lost, at entrances, lift lobbies and corridors.
  • Easy updates: the venue changes the map once and every visitor sees the new version.
  • Low setup effort: start from a floorplan image instead of a full app project.

Where apps can still make sense

Apps can offer features such as continuous live positioning and notifications, which suit venues with frequent repeat visitors. For most large public venues, where visitors come once or occasionally, a no-install QR map covers the main need: finding the destination quickly.

How to choose

  • Mostly first-time or occasional visitors: QR-based browser map.
  • Mostly repeat visitors who want live tracking: consider an app.
  • Limited budget or a quick pilot: start with QR.
