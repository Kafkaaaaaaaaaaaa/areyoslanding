“Where is this department?” The question that eats your reception desk

Hospitals answer the same wayfinding questions all day. Here is why visitors get lost, and five fixes that work without rebuilding anything.

AK
Anas KhanFounder, Areyos
20 Oct 20266 min readHospitals

On this page

    Key takeaways

    • Most wayfinding questions are repeats. The same 10 destinations account for most of them.
    • Name places the way visitors say them, not the way the org chart does.
    • Put directions where the decision happens: entrances, lifts, junctions.
    • A QR code on the wall gives every visitor a reception desk in their pocket.

    Stand near a hospital reception desk (the full picture is in our hospital wayfinding guide) for twenty minutes and you will hear the same four questions: Where is radiology? Where do I pay? Which way to the lift? Is this the right building? Staff answer them politely, hundreds of times, and every answer is a minute not spent on something only a human can do.

    Why people get lost in hospitals

    Hospitals grow in layers. A new wing is added, a department moves, a lift is closed for repair. Nobody planned the whole building as one route, so visitors meet it as a puzzle. Add stress, unfamiliar words and a time-sensitive appointment, and even simple signage fails.

    • Visitors arrive once. They never learn the building, so every trip is a first trip.
    • Labels use internal language. “Radiology”, “OPD Block C” and “Day Care” mean little to a patient who was told “get an X-ray”.
    • Decisions happen at junctions. A sign three corridors back does not help at the junction.
    • Staff are the fallback. When signs fail, people ask, and the desk becomes a help line.

    Five fixes that do not need a rebuild

    1. List the top ten destinations

    Ask the front desk to tally questions for one week. You will find a short list dominates: billing, pharmacy, lab, radiology, the main OPD, the cafeteria. Make those ten effortless first.

    2. Rename for the visitor

    Keep the official name, add the plain one: Radiology (X-ray, CT, MRI). Use the same words on signs, on the appointment slip and on staff scripts.

    3. Fix the decision points

    Walk the route as a stranger and mark every place you hesitated. Those spots, not the corridors in between, are where a sign or a QR code earns its place.

    4. Give staff one script

    “Scan the code on that pillar and choose where you are going.” One sentence, same every time, turns a two-minute explanation into five seconds.

    5. Make directions available before arrival

    Print the QR on appointment slips and SMS reminders. A visitor who has already seen the route walks in with confidence.

The visitor picks a destination and follows the route; you update the plan when a department moves. It does not replace good signage. It covers the gaps signage cannot.

    A note on numbers. The share of questions that are repeats varies by site. Measure your own for a week before you decide what to fix first.
