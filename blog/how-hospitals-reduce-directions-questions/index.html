How hospitals can reduce "where is this department?" questions

By Areyos · 2026-10-23

Ask anyone who works at a hospital reception desk what they get asked most. The answer is almost always directions: where is the lab, which floor is billing, how do I get to the OPD, where is the nearest exit. In a large building, those questions repeat all day and pull staff away from patient-facing work.

Why people get lost in hospitals

  • Buildings grow in phases, so layouts are rarely logical.
  • Visitors are stressed and often visiting for the first time.
  • Signs use department names patients don't know.
  • Lifts, wings and floors differ between blocks.

Practical ways to reduce directions questions

  1. Audit your signage. Walk the route from the entrance to your top five destinations as a first-time visitor. Note every spot where you hesitate.
  2. Track the top questions. Ask reception to tally what visitors ask for over a week. Fix signage for those destinations first.
  3. Use plain names. Add simple labels (for example "Blood tests" beside "Pathology").
  4. Put decision-point signs at lift lobbies and junctions, not just at entrances.
  5. Add a digital map visitors can open on their own phone. A QR code on the wall or signboard lets people get directions without asking anyone.

Why a QR-based map works well in hospitals

Visitors scan with their normal phone camera and the map opens in the browser, so there is nothing to install. They search for a destination and follow a route, including between floors. When layouts change, the venue updates the map once instead of reprinting signs everywhere.
