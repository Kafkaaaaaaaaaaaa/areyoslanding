Indoor navigation cost in India: what to budget for

What drives the cost of indoor navigation in India, from mapping and hardware to maintenance, and the questions to ask before you sign.

AK
Anas KhanFounder, Areyos
21 Oct 20266 min readInsights

On this page

    Key takeaways

    • Cost comes from mapping, hardware, install and upkeep, not just a licence fee.
    • Beacon-based systems add hardware and battery costs; QR-based systems do not.
    • Ask what happens to cost when a room moves or a new wing opens.
    • Compare total cost over three years, not the first quote.

    “How much does indoor navigation cost?” has no single answer, because vendors price differently and venues differ. We will not quote figures here. What we can do is show you what to ask, so quotes are easy to compare.

    What drives the cost

    • Mapping. Turning floor plans into a navigable map. More floors, more cost.
    • Hardware. Beacons, sensors or Wi-Fi upgrades add purchase, installation and battery or power costs.
    • Software. A licence or subscription, and whether an app has to be built and maintained.
    • Printing and installation. QR codes need durable prints and someone to place them.
    • Updates. Rooms move, wings open, departments are renamed.
    • Training and support. Briefing staff and fixing issues after launch.

    Why approach matters

    Hardware-based systems tend to cost more up front and need ongoing maintenance as batteries run down and devices move. QR-based systems avoid that hardware layer, so the main costs are mapping, printing and updates. Each approach has trade-offs, covered in QR code or app?.

    Questions to ask every vendor

    1. What exactly is included in the quote: mapping, install, hosting, support?
    2. Is there hardware, and who replaces it when it fails?
    3. What does an update cost when a department moves?
    4. What is the total cost over three years?
    5. Do you need an app, and who keeps it updated?
    6. Can we see a working pilot of our own venue first?

    Weigh cost against the loss

    A fair comparison sets the cost of wayfinding against the cost of visitors getting lost. Our worked examples and calculator help you estimate that with your own numbers.
