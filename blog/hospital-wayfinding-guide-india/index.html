Hospital wayfinding in India: a practical guide

A practical guide to hospital wayfinding in India: why patients get lost, what good wayfinding includes, and how to improve it step by step.

AK
Anas KhanFounder, Areyos
22 Oct 20268 min readHospitals

On this page

    Key takeaways

    • Wayfinding is a patient-experience issue as much as a signage issue.
    • Plan around the journey: arrive, register, consult, test, pay, leave.
    • Combine good signs, plain language, staff scripts and QR directions.
    • Review what visitors actually ask, and fix those spots first.

    Hospitals in India are busy, multilingual and often grown piece by piece. A patient may arrive by auto, carry a file, bring three relatives and speak a different language from the signage. Wayfinding has to work for all of them.

    What wayfinding includes

    It is more than signs. Good hospital wayfinding combines:

    • Signs and floor markings that are consistent and easy to read.
    • Plain naming for departments and rooms, in the languages patients use.
    • People: reception, guards and volunteers with a clear script.
    • Digital directions that visitors can open before or during a visit.

    Map the patient journey

    List the steps a typical patient takes: arrive and park, register, see a doctor, get tests, pay, collect medicines, leave. Every step is a place and every place is a chance to get lost. Walk the journey yourself, then ask a first-time visitor to walk it while you watch.

    Common trouble spots

    • Entrances that lead to the wrong block.
    • Departments that moved but signs did not.
    • Lifts that serve only some floors.
    • Billing and pharmacy in different corners.
    • Emergency routes that visitors cannot find under stress.

    Plan for language and accessibility

    Use the languages your patients speak, with icons where words fail. Mark step-free routes, ramps and accessible toilets, and make sure wheelchairs and stretchers can follow the route you describe.

    A practical improvement plan

    1. Tally reception questions for a week.
    2. Fix the top ten destinations first.
    3. Rename places in plain language.
    4. Add decision-point signs and QR codes.
    5. Give staff one standard sentence for directions.
    6. Review monthly and update when things move.

    Where QR directions help

    A QR code on appointment slips and at entrances gives each patient directions in their pocket, with no app. See the reception-desk problem and how QR navigation works.

    Not a medical or regulatory guide. Safety signage and emergency routes must follow your hospital's fire-safety and accreditation requirements. This guide covers visitor wayfinding only.
