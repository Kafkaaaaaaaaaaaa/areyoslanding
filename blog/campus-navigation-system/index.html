Campus navigation: helping new students, parents and guests find their way

How colleges and universities can help new students, visiting parents and guests find buildings, rooms and events with simple campus navigation.

AK
Anas KhanFounder, Areyos
16 Oct 20265 min readVenues

On this page

    Key takeaways

    • The busiest wayfinding moments are admissions, orientation and events.
    • Room-level directions matter more than building names.
    • Destination lists change with the timetable, so they must be easy to edit.
    • QR codes on printed schedules and event passes reach people before they arrive.

    A campus is familiar to students by October and baffling to everyone else. Admissions days, open houses, exams, conferences and graduations bring hundreds of unfamiliar faces who all need to find a room by a set time.

    Who gets lost

    • First-year students in their first weeks.
    • Parents and guests at admissions and convocation.
    • Exam candidates from other institutions.
    • Conference delegates and visiting faculty.

    What they need

    • Building names that match what they were told.
    • Room numbers and floors, not just blocks.
    • Routes between blocks, including covered or accessible paths.
    • Event venues that change from one day to the next.

    Make it easy to maintain

    Timetables, labs and event halls change often. Choose a system where a staff member can update a destination quickly, without waiting for a vendor and without reprinting signs.

    Reach people before they arrive

    Print a QR code on admit cards, event passes and the admissions brochure so visitors have directions before they reach the gate. Place codes at gates, block entrances and lift lobbies for those who did not plan ahead. The rollout steps are in launch in a week.

    Start with one event. Pilot navigation for your next admissions day or exam. It is a defined moment with clear success: fewer people asking staff for directions.
