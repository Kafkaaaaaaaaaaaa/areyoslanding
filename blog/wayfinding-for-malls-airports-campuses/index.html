Malls, airports, campuses: how wayfinding needs differ by venue

Hospitals, malls, airports and campuses all have lost visitors, but for different reasons. What to prioritise in each.

AK
Anas KhanFounder, Areyos
15 Sep 20266 min readVenues

On this page

    Key takeaways

    • Every venue has first-time visitors, but their goals differ.
    • Hospitals need calm and clarity; malls need discovery; airports need time awareness.
    • Campuses need event and room-level directions.
    • Start with the busiest entrance in each case.

    “Wayfinding” sounds like one problem. Here are the venue guides in short: hospitals, malls, airports and campuses. It is really several, shaped by who walks in and what they are trying to do.

    Hospitals: reduce anxiety

    Visitors are stressed and often on a deadline. Plain names, short routes and step-by-step directions matter more than anything clever. Accessible routes, such as lifts and ramps, should be easy to choose.

    Malls: help people discover

    A shopper who knows where they are going will find it. The opportunity is the shopper who wants “something to eat” or “a pharmacy”. Category search, store listings and promotions can sit beside directions.

    Airports and stations: respect the clock

    Travellers care about time. Directions to gates, check-in, baggage and transport should be fast and unambiguous, with multilingual labels where possible.

    Campuses: rooms and events

    Students and guests need buildings, lecture rooms and event venues, often on short notice. The destination list changes with the timetable, so easy editing is key.

    What it can cost

    Each venue loses money differently: missed consultations, missed leads, abandoned shopping trips, churned members. Our worked examples put illustrative rupee figures on all four, with the arithmetic shown so you can replace our assumptions with yours.

    What they share

    • Visitors who arrive rarely and never learn the layout.
    • Staff who spend time giving directions.
    • A floor plan that already exists somewhere.

    Where to start

    Pick your busiest entrance, list the ten most-requested destinations, and cover that route well. Expand once you have seen what visitors actually choose.

    Where we are starting. Areyos is commencing in Mumbai, Chhattisgarh and Odisha, beginning with hospitals, malls and campuses.
