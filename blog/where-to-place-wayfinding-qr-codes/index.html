Where to place wayfinding QR codes (and how big to make them)

Placement decides whether QR navigation gets used. Practical rules for location, height, size, contrast and the words printed beside the code.

AK
Anas KhanFounder, Areyos
29 Sep 20264 min readGuides

On this page

    Key takeaways

    • Place codes where visitors decide, not where there is spare wall.
    • Eye-level to chest height, in good light, with a clear margin.
    • Always print a short instruction beside the code.
    • Re-scan every code after install and after any renovation.

    A QR code nobody sees is wasted (new to this? read how QR navigation works first). A code in the wrong spot is worse, because it teaches visitors to ignore the rest. These rules keep placement sensible.

    Place codes at decisions

    • Entrances and reception. The first moment a visitor needs to choose a direction.
    • Lift and stair lobbies. People arrive on a new floor with no idea which way to turn.
    • Major junctions. Anywhere two or more corridors meet.
    • Parking exits. Catch visitors before they walk in the wrong door.

    Height, size and light

    Mount codes roughly between waist and eye height so a phone can be held naturally. A larger code scans from further away; a common rule of thumb is to make the code about a tenth of the intended scanning distance. Test in your own lighting, because glare on laminate is a frequent culprit.

    Contrast and margin

    Dark code on a light background, with a clear blank border around it. Avoid placing codes over busy patterns, glass reflections or curved surfaces.

    Say what it does

    People scan what they understand. Print a line beside the code: Scan to find your way, with an icon of a phone. Use the same wording everywhere, in the local language where it helps.

    Maintain them

    Check codes quarterly. Scan every one, replace any that are damaged, and walk the route again after any renovation.

    Rule of thumb, not a standard. Distance and size guidance varies by printing quality and phone camera, so always test on a few devices.
