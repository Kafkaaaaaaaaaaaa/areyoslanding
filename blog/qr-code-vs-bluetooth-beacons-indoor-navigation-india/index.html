QR Code Indoor Navigation vs. Bluetooth Beacons: Cost & Setup Guide for Indian Hospitals & Malls

How QR code indoor navigation compares with Bluetooth beacons on cost, setup time, upkeep and visitor friction, for Indian hospitals and malls.

AK
Anas KhanFounder, Areyos
14 Nov 20265 min readGuides

On this page

    If you run a hospital or a mall in India and want visitors to find their way indoors, two approaches come up first: QR codes that open a map in the phone’s browser, and Bluetooth beacons that let an app estimate where a phone is. They solve the same problem in different ways, and the difference shows up in cost, setup time, upkeep and how many visitors actually use the result. This guide compares them for Indian hospitals and malls. We build the QR kind, so we also say where beacons are the better choice.

    The short version

    • Choose QR codes when visitors come once or rarely, when you want to go live fast and when you cannot afford to install and maintain hardware across many floors.
    • Choose beacons when you need a live blue dot that follows the person, for example for staff workflows, asset tracking or visitors who all install your app.
    • Many venues need less than they think. Most visitors want the route from where they are standing to one destination.

    How each one works

    QR code navigation. A printed QR code sits on a wall at a known spot, such as the entrance or beside a lift. The visitor scans it with the phone camera, a map opens in the browser and the code tells the system where the visitor is starting. The visitor picks a destination and follows the route step by step. Nothing is installed on the phone or in the building apart from the plaque. The location is the QR code you scanned, not a live position, so scanning another code updates it. Here is a longer walkthrough of how it works.

    Bluetooth beacons. Small battery-powered transmitters are fixed around the building and broadcast a signal. A phone with Bluetooth switched on measures signal strength from several beacons to estimate its position, normally inside an app that the visitor has installed. Accuracy depends on how many beacons there are, where they are fixed and what the building is made of.

    Cost: what each option makes you pay for

    Neither option has a single price. What you pay for is different, and that is what to compare when you collect quotes.

    Cost itemQR code navigationBluetooth beacons
    HardwarePrinted plaquesBeacons, plus batteries or power
    InstallationSticking plaques at chosen spotsMounting units, often with ladders and after-hours access
    Survey and tuningRoute walk-throughSignal survey and re-tuning after layout changes
    Map workMapping from a floor planMapping plus placing each beacon on the map
    UpkeepUpdate the map when a department movesBattery swaps and checks, plus map updates
    New floor or wingMap it and print new plaquesBuy, install and survey again

    Beacon makers note that the price of the unit depends on the chip, battery size, casing and extra sensors, and that bigger batteries cost more but need replacing less often (BeaconZone). One competing vendor argues the larger costs sit around the unit: installation access, battery replacement visits, repeated surveys and expansion (Mapsted). That source sells a rival product, so treat it as a checklist of questions and not a verdict. We are not quoting rupee prices, because they depend on your building. Ask each vendor to split the quote into hardware, labour and five years of upkeep. For a budget checklist on the QR side, see what to budget for indoor navigation in India.

    Setup time

    A QR system needs a floor plan, a mapping pass and printed plaques. For a single building this is a matter of days, and the map can be shown to you before you commit. A beacon system adds purchasing, installation across every floor, a survey and testing, which usually makes it a project with a longer timeline.

    Maintenance

    Beacons are electronic devices that run on batteries, so someone has to check and replace them, and each replacement is a small job in an occupied hospital. QR plaques are printed. They need replacing if they are damaged, and the map changes in software when a ward moves, so the codes on the wall do not need to be reprinted.

    Visitor friction

    This is where the two differ most for hospitals. A beacon experience usually needs an app, Bluetooth switched on and often a location permission. A patient’s family member arriving in a hurry will often not do that. A QR code needs the phone camera that almost everyone has and a browser, with no download or account. If a feature is not used, its accuracy does not matter. We cover the same trade-off for apps in QR code or app?

    Accuracy and live tracking

    Beacons can show a moving position, which a QR code cannot. Their accuracy varies with crowds, furniture and walls, and it can drift as a building changes. QR codes give a precise starting point, but between codes the route is guidance, not tracking. For most visitor journeys of a few minutes, a clear route from a known start is enough, and the visitor can scan another code to reset if they are unsure where they are.

    Privacy

    A QR code does not follow anyone. Areyos collects no personal data from visitors. Beacon systems that locate a phone raise their own questions about permissions and data, which is something to ask any vendor.

    When beacons are the better choice

    • Hospitals with staff-facing apps that need live positions, such as locating equipment or routing porters.
    • Venues with an app visitors already use every day, such as a large mall’s loyalty app.
    • Places where a live blue dot is part of the product, for instance airports serving frequent travellers.

    ROI: how to compare in your own numbers

    Use your own figures and not an industry average.

    1. Count how many direction questions reception and security answer in a day.
    2. Multiply by the minutes each takes and the hourly cost of the staff involved.
    3. Add late or missed appointments caused by getting lost, if you track them.
    4. Compare the total with the five-year cost of each option: hardware, installation, upkeep and updates.

    Our getting lost cost calculator walks through the first three steps.

    Questions to ask any vendor

    1. What exactly is in the quote: hardware, installation, survey, mapping and support?
    2. What happens when a department moves?
    3. Who replaces batteries or plaques, and how often?
    4. What must the visitor install or switch on?
    5. What data does the system collect about visitors?

    Which should you pick?

    Pick QR codes if your visitors are mostly one-time or occasional, you want to launch quickly and you want the lowest upkeep. Pick beacons if you need live positioning and can get people to install and use an app. Some venues start with QR codes and add live positioning later only where it pays for itself.
