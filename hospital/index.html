Indoor navigation for hospitals, with a QR code and no app

Patients and their families rarely know the building. They scan a printed QR code on the wall with their phone camera, pick OPD, the lab, radiology or the pharmacy, and follow the route one step at a time, across floors if needed. There is nothing to download, no account to create and nothing to install in your building. Areyos is starting in Mumbai, Chhattisgarh and Odisha.

    Why hospital visitors get lost

    Hospitals grow in layers. A new wing opens, a department moves, a lift is closed for repair. Most visitors come once, so every visit is a first visit. The people who answer “where is radiology?” all day are your reception staff, security guards and nurses. Our hospital wayfinding guide goes deeper.

    How Areyos works in your hospital

    1. You share a floor plan. A scan or photo of the plan is enough to start.
    2. We map it. We mark destinations, walkways, lifts and stairs, and test the routes against the real building. Setup time depends on the number of floors and destinations. For a single building it is a few hours of mapping.
    3. You place the QR codes. Printed plaques go at the entrance, reception, lifts and key junctions.
    4. Visitors scan and walk. The map opens in the phone’s browser, the visitor picks a destination, and Areyos shows the route with Next and Back buttons.

    Destinations your visitors can choose

    Registration, OPD and consultation rooms, radiology and diagnostics, the laboratory, pharmacy, billing, wards, ICU, lifts and stairs. You decide the list, and only mapped places appear.

    No hardware, no app, no personal data

    • No beacons, sensors or ceiling installs. The only physical items are printed QR plaques.
    • No app for patients. It opens in any phone browser.
    • No sign-up. Areyos collects no personal details from visitors.
    • Works with your signboards. QR plaques add to your existing signage and do not replace it.

    What it means for your team

    Fewer interruptions at reception, fewer late arrivals for appointments and a calmer entrance during OPD rush hours.

    Change the map without reprinting

    When a department moves or a ward is renamed, we update the route. The QR codes on your walls stay the same.

    Questions hospital administrators ask

    Do patients need to install an app?

    No. They scan the wall QR code with their phone camera and the map opens in the browser.

    Do we need beacons or any other hardware?

    No. There are no beacons, tags or sensors. You only need printed QR plaques on the walls.

    Does it cover multiple floors and wings?

    Yes. Routes can include lifts and stairs, and each floor is mapped from your floor plans.

    Does it track patients?

    No. A QR code tells Areyos which spot the visitor is standing at. It does not follow them through the building, and it collects no personal data.

    What happens when we renovate or move a department?

    We update the map and the route. The QR codes stay the same.

    How long does setup take?

    A floor plan and a few hours of mapping are enough to go live for a single building. More floors and destinations take longer.

    What does it cost?

    Pricing depends on the size of the hospital and the plan. We offer free pilots for hospitals, so you can see it on your floor plan first.
